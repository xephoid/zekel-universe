Actions — sixteen things a player does Only the first four are cards. The rest are the choices those cards open, and they earn marks because every one of them already has a screen on the Player choices page and a line in the log. Grouped by where they happen, because that is how a player meets them.
4 CARDS 12 CHOICES
On the stack Three cards in hand, four with a hero
Build A block coming down onto a plinth. One unit, building or base.
Move / Battle An arrow meeting a clash. One card, two outcomes, and the arrow comes first.
Research A branching tree, not a lightbulb. Unlock a technology, or buy a strategy card.
Pass Not a card at all. It is drawn like one so it sits beside them in the hand.
Inside a Move / Battle Three shapes of the same card
Move One tile to one neighbour. The dot is where you left, and it stays visible.
Rally Every neighbour at once, pointing inward. The only mark with no single origin.
Retreat Leaving a wall behind, all of you, one way. The bar is the hex you cannot go back to.
Attack Crossed, with hilts. Entering an occupied tile is what makes this happen, never a button.
Inside a battle Two of these stop the table
Activate One unit takes its turn, in initiative order. The plainest mark, because it happens most.
Commit a card Face down onto the line. Both sides commit before either sees, so the table waits here.
Defend A shield with the hit glancing off. Out of turn, and usually declined.
Reveal the spy The mask lifting. Spends the assignment, so it is the one battle choice you cannot take back.
Diplomacy The intended winning path
Offer a treaty One token crossing to another. Only at the start or end of your own action.
Accept Out of turn, and the only one of the four that creates income for two seats at once.
Decline Costs the offerer a window, not you. The response screen says when their next one is.
Break a treaty A chain parted, with the fracture drawn between the halves. The log always names who broke it.
The four cards are drawn heavier than the twelve choices. A card is an object you hold, place and lose for the round; a choice is something you do once a card is already resolving. The heavier tile is the whole distinction, and it is why Pass is drawn as a card even though no card exists — it occupies the same place in the hand and costs you the same round.
Hero cards add no new mark Marshal Ashlock, Quartermaster Hollis and Archivist Dray each grant a fourth card, but it is a second copy of one of the three. A hero-granted card shows the same mark with the hero’s star in its corner, so nothing new has to be learned.
